# 8x8

## Quick verdict

It is 8:47 on Monday morning. Twice the normal number of customers are waiting for support, one agent is off sick, callbacks are moving too slowly, and some calls still reach an old queue. With a conventional phone system, the hunt for the fault begins. In 8x8, a supervisor can inspect queue pressure, agent status and abandonment together, reassign trained colleagues, then use historical reporting to see whether the intervention worked.

That is the practical case for 8x8: not another video-meeting button, but the connection among business voice, contact-centre routing and operational analysis. We **recommend** it for organisations where customer communication is a measurable service process. It is often too much platform for a small team with ten extensions and little inbound traffic.

## What 8x8 is today

Depending on the package, 8x8 brings together cloud telephony, meetings, messaging, contact-centre capabilities and analytics. Employees call through desktop, mobile or compatible hardware; service teams work with queues, automatic distribution and defined escalation. Reporting covers real-time and historical agent and queue metrics.

A shared platform is not valuable by itself. It matters when a customer interaction no longer falls between the phone system, helpdesk and CRM. Reaching that point requires deliberate design of numbers, roles, integrations and retention.

## A realistic support day

Before launch, the team defines a main queue, an overflow group and a callback path. In the morning the supervisor sees not only that call volume is high, but which queue is growing, how long people are waiting and which trained agents are available. Two colleagues are temporarily moved to support. Critical cases continue in the helpdesk with a call note and a named owner.

The afternoon review does not rely on memory. Historical reporting shows when wait time fell, how many callers abandoned and whether callbacks provided relief. An unusually low average handling time is not automatically good news: calls may have ended early or been categorised poorly. Metrics always need an operational interpretation.

## Who is 8x8 for?

- Organisations with several sites, phone numbers or distributed teams
- Support and sales operations using queues and service targets
- Teams that want to administer telephony, meetings and contact centre together
- Businesses that need traceable CRM or helpdesk handovers
- Organisations with named owners for routing, recording and reporting

It is less suitable when only a few extensions are needed, there is no contact centre, and a simple local or hosted phone service already works reliably.

## Strengths

- One operating model for business voice and contact centre
- Real-time and historical metrics for agents and queues
- Central management of numbers, groups, forwarding and roles
- Desktop, mobile and hardware access for distributed work
- Integrations can pass conversation context to CRM or helpdesk
- More scalable than a collection of unrelated calling and meeting accounts

## Limits and risks

- Rollout and administration are materially more complex than simple VoIP
- Poor routing rules become centralised, not automatically sensible
- Recordings, transcripts and call metadata increase privacy obligations
- Capabilities and availability may vary by package and region
- Internet, device and network quality remain part of actual call quality
- Metrics create bad incentives if staff are judged only by duration or volume

## Workflow fit

Before migration, map the current communication path. Which number leads where? When does overflow start? Who may record calls? Which system owns the customer case? What failover works during a site or internet outage?

A useful pilot is more than a friendly test call. It includes a traffic spike, a team transfer, a callback, a mobile agent, a wrong CRM match and a failover route. Only after these scenarios are observable and recoverable should the number estate move.

## Reporting without metric theatre

8x8 Analytics can provide real-time and historical agent and queue views, configurable reports and exports. That supports staffing and root-cause analysis. A service team should still choose a small number of meaningful measures: wait time, abandonment, availability, repeat contact and a case resolved correctly.

A dashboard cannot replace call sampling or coaching. The best metric leads to a concrete improvement and has side effects the team understands.

## Privacy & operations

Phone numbers, contact details, call metadata, voicemail and recordings may be personal or confidential. Before activation, document purpose, legal basis, notices, consent where required, access roles, retention and deletion. Permissions for recordings and exports deserve especially strict treatment.

Integrations also need data minimisation. Not everyone answering a call requires a complete CRM history, and not every conversation needs permanent storage. European deployments should review current contract, hosting and subprocessor terms directly with the provider.

## Pricing & costs

8x8 is subscription software. Packages, contact-centre options, usage, numbers and contract terms affect the total. Porting, devices, network assessment, implementation, training and continuing administration belong in the business case as well.

## Editorial assessment

**Editorial verdict: Recommend.**

8x8 is compelling when a call is managed as a complete customer journey: routing, conversation, handover, follow-up and improvement. The platform does not become good through activating every capability. It becomes good through a comprehensible number plan and clearly owned queues.

**Editorial verdict:** Recommended for growing service and communications operations with measurable workflows. Use with caution for small teams seeking only an uncomplicated phone system.

## FAQ

**Can 8x8 replace a conventional phone system?**

Yes. Cloud voice can provide numbers, extensions, forwarding and endpoints. Porting, emergency routing and network quality still require advance testing.

**Is 8x8 only for contact centres?**

No. It also covers business telephony and collaboration; contact-centre functions are important but not the whole platform.

**Which metrics should a pilot inspect?**

Wait time, abandonment, available agents, transfers, repeat contacts and correctly resolved cases say more than call volume alone.

**Can 8x8 connect with CRM and helpdesk tools?**

Integrations are a core use case. Decide which data may be read or written and which system remains the owner of the customer case.

**Must calls be recorded?**

No. Recording should be enabled only for a defined purpose with appropriate legal basis, notice, access control and deletion period.

**How should call quality be tested?**

Use realistic networks, headsets, mobile devices, load and sites. A demonstration inside one office is not a sufficient resilience test.

**Does 8x8 suit small businesses?**

It can when several queues, channels or sites need coordination. A leaner service is often more economical for a handful of simple extensions.

**What is the most common rollout mistake?**

Moving an old, unclear call-routing scheme into the cloud unchanged. Clean up processes and ownership before migrating the technology.
